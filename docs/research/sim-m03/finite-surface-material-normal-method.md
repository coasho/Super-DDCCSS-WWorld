# Finite surface material normal

The finite quad rod path must use the authored plate's geometric normal when evaluating the existing plate penetration law. Its in-plane half-planes bound the finite footprint; their lateral normals are not additional material-face orientations. This distinction changes no geometric intersection, timing, thickness, mass, area, fit coefficient, angular limit, collision tolerance or scene input.

Damage.h defines thorResidual's angle as the angle off the plate normal. The point quad path supplies the cross-product quad normal, and the closed-mesh triangle path explicitly supplies each triangle's geometric normal. The rod surface-quad path previously forwarded finiteLineContact's nearest constraint normal, which can instead select an in-plane edge at a face/edge seam. A few ulps in equivalent normal construction could then change the constitutive angle substantially even when the contact point and time agree. Generic closest-feature contact normals and material normals have different roles.

The repair is limited to the surface-quad caller: after the unchanged successful finiteLineContact query it supplies planes.front().normal, which facePlanes constructs from the actual offset material-face corners. It keeps the generic solver and its nearest-constraint output unchanged. Closed solid boxes, components, closed-skin entry/exit selection, mesh triangles, point paths, visitation, response directions, conservation bookkeeping and all public layouts/signatures retain their existing code. Both zero section depth (one face) and positive section depth (two offset faces) use their own existing material-face plane. The section skin mass/area still determines plate thickness; section depth and the existing1e-10m numerical face band are distinct inputs and are not repurposed.

Primary geometry basis: [Pharr, Jakob and Humphreys, PBRT4 §6.5 Triangle Meshes](https://www.pbr-book.org/4ed/Shapes/Triangle_Meshes) separates finite edge tests from the geometric normal formed from surface edges; [§3.11 Interactions](https://www.pbr-book.org/4ed/Geometry_and_Transformations/Interactions) derives surface normals from tangent derivatives. These establish a numerical geometry convention, not a penetration calibration. [Ansys ICEM CFD, Assign Mesh Thickness](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v251/en/icm_help/iedit_assignmeshthick.html) defines shell thickness in the surface-normal direction. Applying that geometric orientation to this SDK's existing plate-angle contract is the engineering inference; no source is claimed to validate this game's rod law at a real free edge.

The existing empirical THOR policy is retained exactly. Its steel/2024-T3 fit and near-grazing bounds are game-model assumptions with their previously documented domain limitations. Correcting an extent/material normal mix does not establish actual free-edge failure, rod deformation, material-specific hardware calibration or positive internal component work.

The method regression uses public assessSceneV1 with a fixed capture-derived affine rod query and one authored F16 lower material face. It evaluates the existing equation independently against that face normal. Point quad and triangulated-prism point controls use the same incoming mean velocity and material inputs; their shared horizon is derived from strict-interior front contact and first unattenuated side exit, so another material wall cannot contaminate the comparison. The main rod's original unit interval remains unchanged. All original test source is preserved as an exact prefix. Actual native RED/GREEN and fresh model outcomes belong to dated receipts, not this method explanation.
