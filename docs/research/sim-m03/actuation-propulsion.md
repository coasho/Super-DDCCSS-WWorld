# Variant actuation and finite inlet methods

## Observable production mechanisms

R-73 and PL-10 select the existing `jsbsim_nozzle_v1` binding. `thrustvector.install` emits actual finite native nozzle pitch/yaw actuators, explicit angle/rate stops, and a physical aft nozzle moment arm. It normalizes the signs against every admitted aerodynamic-control derivative. Native thrust is rotated by JSBSim; zero motor thrust gives zero vectoring force. The shared channel lag applies once. The root owns current card admission and native response gates. The R-73 mechanism is supported by Rosoboronexport's R-73E description of combined aerodynamic/gas-dynamic control; PL-10 TVC is an explicitly uncertain inference in CASI's authored 2020 analysis, not a released manufacturer's numeric actuator map. New equivalent actuator limits/rates remain engineering assumptions with nonzero uncertainty.

AIM-9M's four rollerons are now actual tail-tip hinged control patches in the shared aerodynamic model, each with a stable physical-fin identity. Their generated native aerodynamic tables remain present. Their active mixer gains, command authority and controller derivative contributions are zero. Before each actual native flight substep, the private `PassiveRolleronV1` helper integrates a wheel and hinge using only own air-relative flow, density and inertial angular rate. The resulting bounded physical angle drives that patch's real aerodynamic property. Removing electrical roll authority does not remove passive roll stabilization.

The wind-driven wheel obeys `Jw*omega_dot = Ctau*q*A*r - (Bwind+Bbearing)*omega`, with signed axial flow, `Bwind=abs(Ctau*q*A*r)*r/(lambda*abs(u))`. Its maximum harvested power is bounded by `Ctau*lambda*q*A*abs(u)/4`; the authored wheel drag exceeds that bound and is applied at the physical patch centre. The hinge obeys `Jh*delta_ddot + c*delta_dot + (k+q*ka)*delta = -Jw*omega*p`. The equal roll gyro reaction is integrated on the actual bounded hinge trajectory, with opposite gyro work. Wheel spin-up and hinge angular-momentum changes react onto their bearings; finite stops/rate constraints remove mechanical energy. Angle, wheel and hinge-rate state reset with the owned native model. No target, guidance command, fitted direct body damping or ideal acceleration enters this helper.

This follows the reduced roll/hinge mechanism and damping requirement of [Nason, Brown and Rock, NACA RM SL55C22 (1955)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20090023599), whose primary report was checked. The old research vehicle's values are not AIM-9M calibration. The aerodynamic hinge stiffness is derived from the authored plain-flap geometry: integrate the thin-airfoil pressure moment only aft of its hinge, then apply the declared finite-aspect lifting-line factor `AR/(AR+2)` using one physical full fin's span and area. `hinge-aerodynamic-method=1` selects this closure in both the actual TOML and preset; no free `1e-6 m^3` stiffness remains. The section pressure method follows [NACA Report 938, Flap Theory](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19930092003/downloads/19930092003.pdf). For the current tip patch, the infinite-span coefficient is `0.26645586*c^2*span`; the finite-fin result is approximately `1.60e-4 m^3`. This is an internally consistent reduced-order closure, not measured AIM-9M hinge data. Sweep, finite-wing interference and separated/compressible hinge pressure remain approximations. The current generic wheel/hinge constants, uncertain tip-patch dimensions and shared VLM loads are explicit engineering approximations. Emitted `relative-input-uncertainty=0.5` records a nonzero 50 percent engineering input bound on the generic mechanical inputs, not an equipment confidence interval or independent perturbations that may violate coupled physical admissibility. Each RK stage respects hinge stops/rate constraints, so a stationary blocked hinge generates no viscous heat or gyro work. The helper is a roll/hinge reduction, not a fully coupled six-axis multibody bearing model. Independent gates cover constant-flow wheel spin, forced hinge response, gyro exchange, stops, rate bounds, reverse flow, reset, malformed input and actual native roll response.

## Meteor v2 resident-air control volume

Retained `fsim.propulsion.jsbsim_ducted_rocket.v1` output semantics remain unchanged. V2 adds one positive finite role, `inlet_residence_s` (seconds), to the existing 15 cycle/valve roles. The inlet admits only `rho*A*max(u_air_body,0)` after the own-speed/lightoff and booster boundary. Lateral/reverse speed cannot supply fresh inlet air. The existing finite native fuel valve, reservoirs, grain split, actual native fuel depletion and gross nozzle thrust path remain in use.

Five matched native first-order filters represent a lumped convective transit inventory. Their inputs are captured mass flux, total enthalpy flux `m_in*(cp*T + |v_air|^2/2)` and the three captured momentum fluxes `m_in*v_air`. Outflow is inventory divided by residence time. Thus resident air mass is `tau*m_out`, enthalpy is `tau*H_out`, and relative momentum is `-tau*P_out`. The air mass is an actual native point mass at the derived intake centre. Closure of the inlet drains these finite inventories; lingering outflow cannot create fresh mass or heat.

JSBSim's native lag filter uses the trapezoidal method. Admission must enforce `native_substep <= 2*tau` so a nonnegative captured flux cannot create negative inventory. The emitted `maximum-native-substep-s` metadata records this bound. Matched filters obey the same discrete mass, energy and momentum transport balance. Resident mass is lumped at one point, so internal gas inertia distribution is a model-order approximation.

The explicit V2 native reservation path drains selected usable fuel at native tank priority before the engine evaluates heat or thrust. Equal-priority tanks share demand with bounded redistribution. A fractional final batch reduces native flow, heat and impulse together; subsequent dry ticks clear flow and heat. Property-owned reservation diagnostics and additive nonvirtual helpers preserve the old rocket layout/vtable and the V1 Calculate/ConsumeFuel branch. Fuel freeze or integration suspension reserves no finite V2 mass and therefore supplies no chemical heat. Actual consumed native fuel contributes chemical heat, limited by admitted air/fuel ratio and combustion efficiency. The equivalent fully expanded nozzle uses resident total enthalpy, the declared generic pressure recovery and an isentropic pressure closure. Gross exit momentum passes through the native sustainer engine. Air-only outflow when fuel is absent uses the same native physical reaction path; it cannot burn chemical energy. The full ram/inventory reaction is always applied in body axes, including `-Omega cross P_res`. Native external-reaction pre-functions freeze each actually applied `ram-reaction-{x,y,z}-n` at the force epoch; the real force and producer share that cache. `net-propulsion-force-{x,y,z}-n` adds native current engine body force to that same reaction. These are diagnostics of native execution, not client force reconstruction. This follows the open control-volume momentum balance; the filtered ram term is required because the finite volume owns relative momentum rather than instantaneously attaching all captured air to the airframe.

The equations follow [NASA Glenn's ramjet momentum balance](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/ramth.html), checked directly: exit momentum, captured-air ram momentum and pressure contribution. The chosen zero-area fully expanded equivalent nozzle prevents a second ambient-pressure subtraction. The implementation does not resolve shocks, combustor acoustics, flame chemistry, inlet distortion/unstart hysteresis, nozzle separation or fuel-regression CFD. Pressure recovery, combustion efficiency, capture area, residence time and valve laws are generic uncertain inputs, not a Meteor manufacturer map. [MBDA's official Meteor description](https://www.mbda-systems.com/products/air-dominance/meteor) supports the airbreathing mechanism, not these numeric coefficients.
