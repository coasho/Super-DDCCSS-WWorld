# Weapon contact-fuze initiation

The private Mission Systems helper `weapon::contactFuze` detects a captured missile nose-tip point meeting currently attached aircraft geometry. It supplies an initiation candidate to the SDK's existing detonation path. It does not integrate flight, apply an impact force, calculate kinetic penetration, create a debris outcome or calibrate an equipment fuze. The independent proximity-fuze channel keeps its existing semantics.

## Geometry and frames

`ContactSample::pointBody` is the nose point in the victim's body axes, in metres from its fixed model origin: x forward, y right, z down. Adjacent native samples define linear segments in that relative frame. The helper does not replace the native path with the outer decision-step chord and does not invent an effective missile radius.

The borrowed `damage::AttachedGeometryView` supplies immutable source IDs and excludes separated sections. Surface panels are finite triangles `(0,1,2)` and `(0,2,3)` of their authored corners; this also defines a folded quad without inventing a best-fit plane. Collapsed triangles have no face. Coplanar motion can enter a finite face through its edge. Closed body contact volumes come from the separate versioned authored geometry described below. Damage/penetration boxes cannot authorize physical contact. Skin thickness and aerodynamic area do not enlarge the contact geometry. Boundaries are inclusive; there is no convex hull, sphere or gap-closing tolerance.

The helper checks the currently attached view for the whole supplied sweep. It cannot reconstruct geometry that separated partway through that interval, and it does not test detached bodies. A captured native segment is a linear approximation in the target's rotating frame, rather than a continuous solution for rotational motion between samples. Capture every existing native boundary, including a common endpoint. No extra trajectory integrator is introduced.

An initial point inside/on an attached closed mesh or on a finite surface sets `initialOverlap` and suppresses that victim's entire supplied sweep. This is a conservative refusal to infer an earlier crossing or arm on an exit. An empty corner of a mesh's bounds is outside. Surface panels alone are not a watertight solid-volume test: an initial point enclosed by a panel shell but touching no face or closed volume is not classified as an overlap. On a later interval beginning outside all primitives, a genuinely new crossing can be evaluated, unless the release's contact channel has already been consumed.

## Authored contact geometry version 1

`<aircraft>.damage.json` adds `contact_geometry: {"version":1,"meshes":[...]}`. Each mesh declares `kind` (`panel` or `component`), `index` into the unchanged source array, body-axis `vertices`, integer-index `triangles` and required parallel `triangle_neighbors`. A null neighbor marks authored physical skin; `{kind,index}` marks an artificial ownership cut and identifies the adjacent source owner. A mesh is a closed consistently wound volume, with welded finite vertices, noncollapsed faces, two oppositely directed uses of every edge and a single cyclic triangle fan at every vertex. The reader rejects malformed/unknown explicit versions, missing/mismatched provenance, invalid/self neighbor identities, invalid or duplicate mesh identities, noninteger/out-of-range indices, open/non-manifold topology, unused vertices and zero/overflowing volume. It does not repair reader data or fall back to a damage box. Legacy files without this key remain readable by the damage chain, but contact is unavailable (`valid=false`). The v1 format was completed before its first commit; earlier uncommitted v1 products must be regenerated rather than interpreted without provenance.

`setContactGeometryV1` seals validated immutable geometry, source lookup tables and bounds once. Bounds derive from the mesh vertices, since an authored shape can extend beyond a midpoint damage box. A segment/point bounds rejection precedes triangle work. Bounds overlap alone never initiates; closed triangles provide the narrow phase. Point-on-triangle inclusion precedes the signed-solid-angle sum. The implementation uses `2*atan2(det(a,b,c),1+a·b+b·c+c·a)` for unit directions and classifies the absolute winding above one half as inside, following equations 5–6 of [Jacobson, Kavan and Sorkine-Hornung (2013), section 4.1](https://igl.ethz.ch/projects/winding-number/robust-inside-outside-segmentation-using-generalized-winding-numbers-siggraph-2013-compressed-jacobson-et-al.pdf). No dependency, surface repair or physical-distance tolerance is added. Validation checks topology and finite arithmetic; it is not a general self-intersection solver for arbitrary imported meshes.

The maintained `hangar.damage.contact_geometry(aircraft, origin, existing_panels)` is independent of mass, strength and aerodynamic recomputation. It samples the existing authored `Body.skin` at that method's default resolution, selects each mirrored copy independently, clips its triangles only to the existing longitudinal panel-ownership interval, and closes the cut with a planar cap. Deterministic five-decimal welding and exact integer zero-area removal handle legitimate collapsed tip rings before closed-edge validation. Geometry retains the existing panel index/section, including mounted pods, and detached membership uses the existing immutable view. Adjacent closed intervals meet at their cut; the cut never enlarges their outer cross-section. Generated cut triangles name the same-copy adjacent interval's source panel. Original authored blunt-end caps remain physical skin. The generator emits no contact volume for internal vulnerability boxes. A deliberately exact synthetic box/component fixture explicitly marks every real face with a null neighbor.

Containment always includes every closed-volume face, including ownership cuts. Narrow-phase contact hides a cut while its adjacent source owner remains in the attached view, even if that owner's physical mesh is unavailable. When the neighbor is detached/absent, the cut is an exposed separation face. A hidden cut cannot initiate or provide an impact normal. At an exact event-time tie, authored skin wins over an exposed cut before the existing primitive/identity ordering, including a shared native-segment endpoint. This preserves a genuine outer-skin normal at ownership rims.

Only the additive key is regenerated in existing products. Their original panel/component/strength/warhead values and fragment/blast assessment remain unchanged. This is authored tessellated geometry at five-decimal coordinate precision, not exact analytic curved skin or calibrated equipment contact sensitivity. Native sample segmentation, open lifting-surface sheets and the existing detached-body limitation still apply.

## Time and release state

`ContactWindow` gives the age at the start of the original World decision step, its full duration, inclusive arming age, exclusive life age and a cutoff within that step. A native terrain arrest or divergence uses an exclusive cutoff. Ordinary captured end-of-step contact can use an inclusive cutoff. A history that stops earlier is not extrapolated. Empty/single-point paths, nonzero first offsets, non-increasing offsets, unavailable samples and non-finite inputs reject the sweep. An unavailable intermediate sample invalidates the supplied sweep; it must never be bypassed with an outer chord.

For each exposed finite primitive the helper obtains its first segment intersection, then selects the earliest physical crossing across all supplied segments and primitives before applying arming. Exact ties prefer authored skin to ownership cuts, then a panel to a component, then the lower immutable source index. `offsetS` and `fraction` locate that crossing in the original decision step. `pointBody` is the selected triangle intersection in target body metres from its fixed model origin. `normalBody` is that triangle's unit normal, oriented against the incoming captured segment. The normal comes from the selected folded-face triangle, rather than a quad average or internal cap. Coplanar entry may have a normal but zero normal closing movement, which supplies no normal collision impulse by itself. No-contact results leave point/normal unavailable as NaNs. Life and cutoff checks exclude later events. Arithmetic overflow, or a selected event that cannot be represented inside an exclusive endpoint after conversion to the returned time/fraction, fails closed with `valid=false`.

`contact=true, triggered=false` records a valid first contact before arming. The caller must still commit this crossing. `ContactFuzeState` belongs to one released weapon incarnation, survives decision steps and is reset only with that release's lifecycle. `commit` consumes the contact channel exactly once, including an unarmed first crossing. Invalid, initial-overlap and no-contact results do not consume it. Consuming an unarmed crossing does not end the weapon, add a new `WeaponEnd` value or disable the separate proximity channel. The helper does not infer an inert collision's physical consequences.

Per-target evaluation is pure. Evaluate all admitted victims before committing anything; select the lowest contact time globally, using stable entity identity for equal target times and the helper's primitive priority within a victim. Commit only this result. Otherwise target iteration order could consume a later contact before an earlier one. Once consumed, skip contact-channel evaluation in later steps. Existing proximity initiation and contact initiation use the same existing detonation authority; the World must choose an earlier armed initiation candidate before issuing a single detonation. An earlier unarmed contact consumes only the contact channel.

## Exact authored nose and native integration seam

Generated store models expose an additive versioned property set:

* `fsim/model/nose-version = 1`: a supported, unique authored point tip.
* `fsim/model/nose-x-in`, `fsim/model/nose-y-in`, `fsim/model/nose-z-in`: its absolute JSBSim structural coordinates in inches, x aft, y right, z up.
* Version zero or missing/unsupported metadata makes this contact channel unavailable. Do not substitute half-length, launch CG or a guessed sensor radius.

The generator source is `tools/models/hangar/hangar/storebodies.py::point_contact_nose`. It applies each authored body's origin to its first station and selects the unique foremost point tip. This includes a separately authored infrared dome ahead of a blunt fuselage. A blunt, mirrored, non-finite or ambiguous foremost body is unavailable. The property block is emitted after `storecontrol.apply`, which replaces the flight-control block. The runtime fixed-origin properties are `fsim/model/origin-{x,y,z}-in`; all three nose coordinates must be available and finite. World binds the validated fixed-origin nose vector for an actual non-jettisoned guided release with an admitted warhead.

For structural nose `n`, fixed structural model origin `o`, and the current `FlightModel::cgFromOriginBodyM` offset `c`, the nose's body displacement from the current CG is:

```text
noseFromOriginBodyM = 0.0254 * (o.x - n.x, n.y - o.y, o.z - n.z)
noseFromCgBodyM     = noseFromOriginBodyM - c
noseEcef           = weaponCgEcef + weaponBodyToEcef * noseFromCgBodyM
targetOriginEcef   = targetCgEcef - targetBodyToEcef * targetCgFromOriginBodyM
pointBody          = transpose(targetBodyToEcef) * (noseEcef - targetOriginEcef)
```

The existing `VehicleState` is located at the current CG. Store CG can move while its motor burns or point masses change. World captures both missile and victim CG offsets in `Entry::engagementCg` at the same native boundaries as `engagementStates`/`engagementPath`; using the final batch CG for every historical pose gives the wrong nose and target-origin paths. An optional value records availability at each index. `World::engagementCgAt` takes an offset from the current World decision-step start, in seconds, and returns that offset from fixed model origin to CG in body metres, linearly interpolated only between matching captured times. Missing/invalid histories return unavailable. Only a natively stopped body's last captured CG can be held beyond its history. The helper never reads the model's current CG as a historical substitute.

Relative samples use the merged native timestamps of both bodies. A flying victim's shorter history bounds the sweep; an already stopped victim retains its pose and CG under the existing stopped-body convention. For a weapon that crashes or diverges, contact evaluation excludes the last interval ending at the corrected/stopped or divergent endpoint. If the optional native terrain snapshot is available, its incoming epoch further bounds this channel at the start of the native step that first reported the contiguous structural contact. A victim newly crashing/diverging in the batch also bounds the encounter at its last preceding native sample. These conservative discrete cutoffs prevent correction jumps from manufacturing a crossing; they are not continuous terrain time-of-impact solutions. The proximity channel retains its existing cutoff convention.

The root-owned World integration uses the following private seams without changing public struct layouts or primary plugin vtables:

1. `World::Entry`: own one `ContactFuzeState`, immutable validated nose metadata and per-native-sample CG data for each actual guided release. A separate release starts with fresh state. `resetVehicle` discards transient motion/CG histories while preserving a consumed channel for that same release. Inert/jettisoned stores do not gain guidance or an initiation channel implicitly.
2. `World::preStep` and the existing final native-sample append: capture current CG offsets at the same times as position/attitude. Bind/read optional metadata through existing FlightModel properties; unsupported models remain explicitly unavailable.
3. `World::finishEngagements`: while the contact channel is unconsumed, evaluate actual attached geometry of admitted Aircraft victims using captured relative nose samples. Do not use seeker tracking/holding as proof of physical contact or restrict a physical crossing to the held designation. Retain existing release/warhead authority and victim policy. Reject malformed histories and apply weapon arming, life, native arrest and divergence cutoffs.
4. Select the earliest physical contact across this weapon's admitted victims before committing it. Compare an armed contact candidate with the existing proximity candidate by event time; exact initiation-time ties preserve proximity priority. Feed at most one armed initiation into the existing `Detonation`, `engagementStateAt`, `World::detonate` and `endWeapon(...Detonated...)` path. A contact detonation event uses `reason="contact"` and the physical victim ID, while the weapon retains its strike target and last seeker holding. Its `missM` is the burst reference's distance from the physical victim. The nose detects contact; the existing warhead burst reference remains the existing weapon reference, rather than being silently moved to the tip. Separate weapons retain the existing within-step weapon-ID processing order; global fractional-time damage ordering is not implemented here.
5. Capture an independent native production regression, including a real generated nose tip, nonzero CG offset and an attached finite panel hit. Prove an unarmed entry followed by a later armed exit does not initiate through this channel, and preserve existing proximity/terrain behavior and installed SDK execution.

## Validation boundary

`tests/simulation/test_contact_fuze.cpp` contains synthetic independent geometry and timing fixtures for first box entry, finite face gaps/edges, coplanar entry, piecewise paths, exact arming/life/cutoff endpoints, initial overlap, missing samples, stable source IDs, separated geometry, malformed inputs, cross-step channel consumption and folded/degenerate faces. These are software invariants; no equipment contact sensitivity, initiator delay, nose crush response or damage effectiveness is inferred from them.

The subsequent tapered-body regressions first failed behaviorally against the original implementation: **6/6 cases failed, 11/45 assertions**, including a false bound-corner contact, wrong true-face time, false initial overlap, legacy availability and ignored malformed metadata (`build/architecture-reviews/sim-m03-five-km-contact-red.log`). Further cases cover mesh-derived bounds, thin volumes, genuine inter-body gaps and validation atomicity. Generator tests exercise collapsed cone tips, closed longitudinal cuts and independent mirrored/translated source membership. Current post-repair native/generator results remain pending until the feature owner records them; historical results below do not verify the new geometry.

The resumed physical-response work adds `[contact-normal]` cases for actual outer skin versus an ownership rim, hidden cuts, exposed separation faces, folded forward/reverse normals, coplanar zero closing, exact selected points and malformed provenance. Root captured their behavioral RED together with an independent momentum-only loading case in `build/architecture-reviews/sim-m03-physical-red-core.log`: **0/7 cases passed, 45/78 assertions failed**. The combined totals include the separate loading case; they are not counts for this helper alone. The point/normal seam supplies geometry to the independently implemented physical response; it does not count an impulse as a fragment or claim functional destruction.

The first combined contact-helper/contact-energy gate passed 17 cases and 162 assertions, including 15 pure contact-helper cases. The native positive fixture subsequently reached genuine behavioral RED in `build/architecture-reviews/sim-m03-sdk-contact-native-red.log`: generated nose metadata, real release/acquisition/loss and the independent one-millimetre placement checks passed, then the expected lost-seeker physical-contact detonation was absent before World integration.

`tests/simulation/test_contact_fuze_native.cpp` exercises actual Host releases and generated JSBSim models with unchanged physical coefficients and arming. Named mechanism fixtures position the independently reconstructed tip over an actual finite outboard F-16 wing panel. The armed case retains the naturally reached propellant fraction to exercise nonzero CG; worker counts one/two and native frame skips one/eight are covered. Negative cases construct a real unarmed crossing followed by an armed crossing on the same release, inert jettison, finite-geometry near miss and a real separated wing body. The fixture converts ECEF to JSBSim's radial ASL initial-condition convention rather than confusing it with ellipsoidal normal height. Its placement tolerance remains one millimetre.

The integrated production fixture now passes all six native cases on Headless and Release. Installed C++, C99 and Python release/control consumers also pass on both presets. Exact current regression and fixed-scene hit outcomes are in the [delivery record](sim-m03-reference-continuation.md#7-sdk-delivery-continuation-and-regression-repair). These fixtures prove initiation and lifecycle behavior; they do not substitute for an independently required actual damage event or calibrated lethality.
